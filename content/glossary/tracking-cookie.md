## What is a tracking cookie?

A tracking cookie is a small piece of data stored in a user's browser after they click a [publisher](publisher)'s [tracking link](tracking-link). It lets the advertiser recognise, when a purchase later happens, which publisher's link the customer originally clicked, even if the sale doesn't happen on the same visit.

## How it works

- **Set on click** — the cookie is placed the moment someone clicks the tracking link.
- **Valid for a set window** — it remains active for the offer's [cookie period](cookie-period), which can range from a few hours to several weeks.
- **Read at purchase** — if the customer buys within that window, the cookie tells the advertiser's system which publisher to credit.

## Limitations

Cookie-based tracking depends on the browser accepting and keeping the cookie, so it can be affected by cleared cookies, private browsing, or cross-device journeys (for example, clicking on a phone and buying on a laptop). This is one reason advertisers increasingly pair cookies with other methods, such as [click ID](click-id) matching, to make [attribution](attribution-window) more reliable.
