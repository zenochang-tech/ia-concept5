## What is an indirect order?

An indirect order is a conversion attributed to a publisher through an attribution rule other than the customer's final click, such as a stored tracking cookie from an earlier visit, or a platform-level [attribution type](attribution-type) that credits more than one touchpoint.

## Indirect order vs direct order

- **[Direct order](direct-order)** — the customer clicked a publisher's link and completed the purchase in the same session, with no other publisher involved.
- **Indirect order** — another interaction sits between the click and the sale, for example the customer left and returned later, or another publisher's link was also involved.
- **[Last-click attribution](last-click-attribution)** — most platforms default to crediting the last valid click, which is what usually decides whether an order counts as direct or indirect.

## Why it matters

Understanding indirect orders helps publishers and advertisers make sense of reporting, especially when [deduplication](deduplication) rules decide which single publisher gets credit when more than one was involved in the journey.
