## What is return on ad spend (ROAS)?

Return on ad spend (ROAS) is a metric that measures the revenue generated for every dollar spent on advertising. A ROAS of 5, for example, means $5 in revenue was generated for every $1 spent. It is a common way advertisers judge whether a campaign or channel is profitable.

## How it is used

- **Comparing channels** — advertisers compare ROAS across affiliate, paid search, and social channels to see where budget performs best.
- **Setting targets** — a minimum ROAS is often used as the bar a campaign must clear to keep running.
- **Alongside [GMV](gross-merchandise-value-gmv)** — ROAS looks at efficiency, while GMV looks at total sales volume.

## Why it matters in affiliate marketing

Affiliate and [performance marketing](performance-marketing) models, where advertisers pay only on results such as [cost per action](cost-per-action-cpa), tend to produce a naturally strong ROAS, since spend is tied directly to outcomes rather than paid upfront regardless of results.
