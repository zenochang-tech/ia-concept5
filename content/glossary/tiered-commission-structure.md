## What is a tiered commission structure?

A tiered commission structure is a type of [commission structure](commission-structure) where the [commission rate](commission-rate) increases as a [publisher](publisher) reaches higher thresholds of sales or volume within a set period, such as a month. Each threshold is a [commission tier](commission-tier), and moving up a tier raises the rate on qualifying actions.

## How it works

- **Base tier** — a standard rate applies from the first sale.
- **Higher tiers** — crossing a sales or volume threshold (for example, $10,000 in a month) unlocks a higher rate.
- **Applies going forward, or retroactively** — depending on the offer, the higher rate may apply only to sales after the threshold, or to the whole period once it's reached.

## Why advertisers use it

Tiered structures reward publishers who drive meaningfully more volume, encouraging them to prioritise the offer over competing ones. For publishers, it means growing an offer's traffic can directly increase the rate earned, not just the total number of sales, similar in effect to a [bonus commission](bonus-commission) but built into the standard rate card.
