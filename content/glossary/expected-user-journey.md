## What is the expected user journey?

The expected user journey describes the typical path a customer follows after clicking a publisher's link on a given [offer](offer-page): landing on a specific page, browsing, and completing a purchase or another qualifying action.

## Why it's documented

- **Setting expectations** — publishers can see where traffic should land, such as a category page or a specific [landing page](landing-page), rather than guessing.
- **Spotting tracking issues** — if actual behaviour deviates from the expected journey, such as clicks not reaching checkout, it can point to a tracking problem rather than a lack of interest.

## Why it matters

Comparing real traffic against the expected user journey helps explain gaps in the [conversion flow](conversion-flow), such as where visitors are dropping off, and gives publishers a clearer picture of how their traffic should behave on a well-tracked offer.
