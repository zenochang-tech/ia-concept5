## What is average order value (AOV)?

Average order value (AOV) is [total sales](total-sales) divided by the number of orders over a given period. It shows how much a typical customer spends per transaction, rather than how many transactions happen.

## Why it matters

- **For advertisers** — a rising AOV often means customers are buying more per visit, which can offset a lower [conversion rate (CVR)](conversion-rate-cvr).
- **For publishers** — offers with a higher AOV can be more valuable to promote even at a modest commission rate, since a percentage-based [commission rate](commission-rate) scales with order size.
- **For comparing offers** — AOV, alongside commission rate and [gross merchandise value (GMV)](gross-merchandise-value-gmv), gives a fuller picture of an offer's earning potential than commission rate alone.

## Example

If an advertiser records $10,000 in sales across 200 orders in a month, the average order value is $50. A publisher earning a 5% commission on that offer would expect roughly $2.50 in commission per order, on average.
