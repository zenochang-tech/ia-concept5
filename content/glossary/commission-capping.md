## What is commission capping?

Commission capping is when an [advertiser](advertiser) sets a maximum [commission](commission) amount that can be earned per sale, per order, or per period, regardless of how high the actual sale value is. It protects the advertiser's margin from unexpectedly large payouts on unusually high-value transactions.

## How it works

- **Per-order cap** — commission on any single order stops increasing past a set amount. For example, a 10% rate on a $2,200 order would normally pay $220, but with a $50 per-order cap, the payout stops at $50.
- **Per-publisher cap** — a limit on the total commission one publisher can earn from the offer.
- **Time-based cap** — a ceiling on total commission the advertiser pays out across a day, week, or campaign period.

The specific rule applied is described by the offer's [capping type](capping-type).

## Why it matters

Publishers promoting high-value products should always check whether commission capping applies, since the effective commission rate can differ substantially from the advertised percentage on large sales. Cap details appear in the offer's description on the [offer page](offer-page).
