## What is brand bidding?

Brand bidding is when a [publisher](publisher) uses an [advertiser](advertiser)'s brand name, or close variations of it, as keywords in [paid search traffic](paid-search-traffic) campaigns. The publisher's ad then appears when someone searches for the brand directly, often competing with the advertiser's own search ads.

## Why it is restricted

- **Higher ad costs** — bidding on a brand name can drive up the cost of search ads for the advertiser itself.
- **Unfair credit** — the publisher earns commission on a customer who was already looking for the brand, not one they genuinely introduced.
- **Trademark concerns** — using a brand name in an ad can raise legal and brand-safety issues.

## On Involve Asia

Brand bidding is one of the most common items listed under an offer's [prohibited promotion methods](prohibited-promotion-methods). Advertisers typically require publishers to add the brand name as a [negative keyword](negative-keyword) in their campaigns, and breaching this rule can lead to [commission capping](commission-capping), clawback, or removal from the offer under the platform's [compliance](compliance) rules.
